under the hood GitHub

Explainer videos, drawn and narrated by Claude Code.

Give it a topic like "what happens when you git push", or point it at your code. You get a 1080p video with hand-drawn animation, a voice, music and sound effects. Everything runs on your machine.

Install the skill View the code

Install it in Claude Code

/plugin marketplace add Rajesh-Royal/under-the-hood
/plugin install under-the-hood@under-the-hood

Then ask for a video:

/under-the-hood:under-the-hood what happens when you git push

Or just say it in plain words, like "make an under-the-hood video about how HTTPS works". Prefer plain skills? Copy skills/under-the-hood and skills/under-the-hood-setup from the repo into ~/.claude/skills.

What happens after you ask

  1. 1

    It checks your setup

    The first run installs what's missing and asks before any brew or apt command. After that it's a one-line check.

  2. 2

    It asks a couple of questions

    Only what it can't guess: which angle on a broad topic, and which voice to use.

  3. 3

    You approve the script

    You read the narration and the storyboard before anything gets rendered. Changing words here is free.

  4. 4

    It draws the scenes

    Claude writes the animation, screenshots every scene and fixes overlaps on its own.

  5. 5

    You pick the voice takes

    Two takes per line, generated on your machine. Listen and pick, line by line if you like.

  6. 6

    It renders the video

    A 1080p MP4 with music that ducks under the voice, sound effects on every beat, and loudness set for posting.

Orange steps are where you weigh in.

Use your own voice

Record 20 to 30 seconds on your phone in a quiet room, talking the way you'd explain something to a friend. The model copies how you speak, not only how you sound. If you'd rather read something, the skill hands you this:

Okay, so here's the thing about the internet. Every time you open a page, your browser quietly sends out dozens of tiny requests. Most of them come back in a fraction of a second, and you never notice. It's kind of wild, right?

Your recording never leaves your machine. Only clone your own voice, or one you have permission to use. Every generated clip carries an inaudible watermark.

How it works

Frames are code
Each video is a small canvas page, rendered frame by frame in headless Chrome. The same script gives the same pixels every time.
The voice sets the pace
Every scene stretches to fit its narration, so editing one line re-syncs the whole video.
Sound is synthesized
Music and effects are generated with Web Audio on the same timeline as the pictures. No sample files and no licenses to worry about.
Mixed for posting
The music ducks under the voice and the mix is normalized to -16 LUFS, which is what X, YouTube and Instagram expect.

Tested on macOS with Apple Silicon. Linux and WSL should work. It needs ffmpeg, Node 18 or newer and uv, and the setup step installs them with your okay. Narration takes about 2 GB of disk for the voice model.
